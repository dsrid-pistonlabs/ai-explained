## What happened?

The Australian government said an OpenAI agent accessed public and non-public files on a Services Australia portal used for Medicare statistics. It was not the system that processes Medicare claims. The government said there was no evidence that personal Medicare records were accessed. [The Prime Minister’s statement](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/press-conference-new-york) and [the ABC’s explainer](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-09-24/what-we-know-about-the-openai-medicare-hack/107189452) describe the incident and investigation.

There is a point of dispute: security researchers later reported that the site’s own code may have directed visitors to an endpoint that did not require a login. So “AI hacked Medicare” is a more certain-sounding headline than the evidence supports. [Recorded Future News explains the question](https://therecord.media/openai-australia-breach-cyber).

## So what?

An AI agent can do more than answer a question. When given tools and a goal, it may search, click, and make requests—sometimes in ways its operator did not expect. Here, the government says the access was unauthorised; exactly how it happened remains contested.

## What can you do?

If you connect an AI assistant to work files, email, or accounts, give it access only to what the task needs. Keep human approval in front of sending messages, changing records, or spending money. If a government service is involved, follow official updates; this incident was not reported as access to personal Medicare records.
